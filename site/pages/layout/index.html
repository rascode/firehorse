03 Masthead

The head of the page

A glass header for an app or a record page, or a lockup that signs off a hero: the wordmark on one etched line with the headline.

Masthead

The head of a page or app. The header is a glass panel with a large caption: number left in the tone, title right, an etched rule under both. --bar is the one-line top bar, the client's round logo first (__mark); --split puts the caption and the tools in two pills with the page between them. The lockup is a hero's sign-off: the wordmark, an etched line from it to the edge, and the headline split by the line, its rolling word in the root colour's family. --app is the app bar on the sidebar's frost panel: a lead with the sidebar cube, summary chips with cards, and tools. Three layouts of it share that panel and content: centred (as is), --app-inline (a one-line readout after the lead, etched rules between items) and --app-tiles (each item a sidebar row tile with its cube, the tools past an etched rule). --app-crumbs puts a crumb trail where the lead was, with no summary: an area crumb that opens the areas and one record crumb that opens a switcher; two levels at most, chevrons on hover only. Shown over a picture so the frost reads.

04 Backdrop

What sits behind the page

A quiet picture behind a page or an app, a blueprint drafting sheet, a picker for the backdrop, the frost that glass takes over it, and a frost line that clears a hero's lower half for reading.

Backdrop

A quiet abstract picture behind a whole page or app: soft tone fields under the sign-in page's etched contours, fixed to the viewport, or a blueprint sheet with data-fh-backdrop="blueprint". --fh-backdrop-strength sets how much tone or grid shows. A grid of option tiles with data-fh-backdrop-select picks the backdrop and remembers it. Glass over it frosts by --fh-frost (0–1), a theme token; a range with data-fh-frost-select dials it live and remembers it. The frost line frosts the art behind a hero from a line down, with a hard edge: --fh-backdrop-frost-from places the line.
