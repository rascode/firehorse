Apple · 2025

Apple Human Interface Guidelines

Apple's top-level design guidance for every Apple platform, grouped into foundations, patterns, components, inputs and technologies.

The index that every other Apple entry hangs off. It splits guidance into Foundations (accessibility, color, layout, materials, typography, motion) and Components, which maps closely onto our chapter split between tokens and anatomy. Use it as the root citation when a Rascode rule follows Apple in general.

Linked from the HIG home as the first of the design fundamentals. Cite it when the Rascode HIG states its own principles, such as content first and controls that support rather than compete with it.

Apple

Apple Human Interface Guidelines: Layout

Apple's guidance on consistent, adaptive layouts that respect safe areas, margins and hierarchy.

Keep content inside safe areas and system margins, extend backgrounds to the edges, and put the most important items in the upper, leading part of the screen. Keep a minimum tappable area of 44x44 pt for every control. Group related items with negative space, background shapes, materials or separators, and use indentation and alignment to show hierarchy. On tvOS, keep primary content 60 px from top and bottom and 80 px from the sides.

Apple

Apple Human Interface Guidelines: Typography

Apple's rules for legible type, text styles and Dynamic Type.

Default and minimum text sizes per platform: iOS/iPadOS 17 pt default and 11 pt minimum; macOS 13 and 10; tvOS 29 and 23; visionOS 17 and 12; watchOS 16 and 12. Avoid Ultralight, Thin and Light weights at small sizes. Use built-in text styles so hierarchy survives when people change text size, and make layouts adapt to every Dynamic Type size, including the accessibility sizes.

Apple · 2025

Apple Human Interface Guidelines: Color

Apple's guidance on semantic system colors, appearance modes and inclusive color.

Never use one color to mean two things. Give every custom color light, dark and increased-contrast variants. Name colors by purpose, not appearance: label, secondary label, separator, and primary, secondary and tertiary backgrounds for nested grouping. Don't rely on color alone to carry meaning. With Liquid Glass, tint only the one primary action's background, not several controls.

Apple · 2025

Apple Human Interface Guidelines: Materials

Apple's guidance on Liquid Glass and standard blur materials for depth and layering.

Liquid Glass is a separate functional layer for controls and navigation that floats above content. Keep it out of the content layer, where standard materials (ultra-thin, thin, regular, thick) belong. Thicker materials give better contrast for fine text, and thinner ones keep context visible. Over bright media, Apple suggests a dark dimming layer of about 35% opacity behind clear glass. Put vibrant label colors on top of materials, never plain grays.

Apple · 2025

Apple Human Interface Guidelines: Motion

Apple's guidance on purposeful, brief, optional motion.

Add motion only when it has a purpose, keep feedback animations brief and precise, and avoid animating interactions that happen often. Motion must be optional: never make it the only way information is conveyed, and let people cancel it rather than wait for it to finish. In visionOS, avoid sustained oscillation near 0.2 Hz and motion at the edges of the field of view.

Apple

Apple Human Interface Guidelines: Accessibility

Apple's accessibility requirements for control size, contrast, spacing and motion.

Minimum control sizes: 28x28 pt on iOS/iPadOS and watchOS, 20x20 pt on macOS, 56x56 pt on tvOS, and 60x60 pt targets in visionOS. Contrast follows WCAG AA: 4.5:1 for text up to 17 pt and 3:1 for 18 pt and larger or bold text. Leave about 12 pt of padding around bezeled controls and about 24 pt around unbezeled ones. With Reduce Motion on, replace axis transitions with fades and cut back on automatic and repeating animation.

Apple · 2025

Apple Human Interface Guidelines: Buttons

Apple's anatomy and usage rules for buttons: style, content and role.

A button needs a hit region of at least 44x44 pt, or 60x60 pt in visionOS. Custom buttons always need a press state. Limit prominent buttons to one or two per view, and use style rather than size to mark the preferred option. Roles are normal, primary, cancel and destructive, and a destructive action must never get the primary role. Start text labels with a verb, and add a trailing ellipsis when the button opens another view.

Apple · 2023

Apple Human Interface Guidelines: Pickers

Apple's guidance on pickers for choosing from medium-to-long lists of values.

Use a picker for medium-to-long lists, a pull-down button for short lists, and a list or table for very long ones. Order values predictably, for example alphabetically, so people can guess the hidden values. Show the picker in context near the field being edited rather than switching views. Coarser minute steps that divide 60 evenly, such as 15, are allowed.

Apple · 2023

Apple Human Interface Guidelines: Segmented controls

Apple's rules for linear sets of equal-width segments that select or act.

Use no more than about five to seven segments in a wide interface, and about five on iPhone. Keep segment widths equal, use either text or images but not both, and label segments with nouns in title case. Never mix selection segments with action segments in one control. For switching whole app sections, use a tab bar instead.

Apple · 2023

Apple Human Interface Guidelines: Lists and tables

Apple's guidance on presenting rows of data in lists and multicolumn tables.

Keep row text short to avoid truncation. When space is tight, consider truncating in the middle so both ends stay recognizable. Use nouns in title case for column headings, with no ending punctuation. Grouped styles separate sections with headers, footers and extra space, and alternating row colors help in wide multicolumn tables. Persistently highlight the selected row in navigational lists.

Material Design 3: Easing and duration

Material 3's easing curves and duration tokens.

M3 defines standard easing at cubic-bezier(0.2, 0, 0, 1), plus emphasized decelerate (0.05, 0.7, 0.1, 1) for entering and emphasized accelerate (0.3, 0, 0.8, 0.15) for exiting. Duration tokens step from short (50 to 200 ms) through medium (250 to 400 ms) and long (450 to 600 ms) to extra-long (700 to 1000 ms). Small, simple changes get short durations, and large surfaces get longer ones.

W3C Accessibility Guidelines Working Group · 2023

Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2

The W3C Recommendation that defines testable web accessibility success criteria.

The normative standard behind every accessibility rule in our HIG. Aim for Level AA, which 2.2 extends with target size minimum (2.5.8) and focus not obscured (2.4.11). Cite specific criteria through the Understanding entries below.

Accessibility

W3C Accessibility Guidelines Working Group · 2023

Understanding WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.3: Contrast (Minimum)

W3C's explanation of the AA text contrast requirement.

Text needs a contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1, and large text (18pt, or 14pt bold, about 24px or 18.66px bold) needs at least 3:1. Logos and disabled controls are exempt. This is the floor for every text and background pair in our color tokens.

ColorAccessibilityType

W3C Accessibility Guidelines Working Group · 2023

Understanding WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.11: Non-text Contrast

W3C's explanation of the AA contrast requirement for UI components and graphics.

Visual information needed to identify a control and its states, such as borders, focus rings and checkbox marks, needs at least 3:1 against adjacent colors. The same goes for meaningful parts of graphics. This sets the floor for our border, separator-as-boundary and icon tokens.

W3C Accessibility Guidelines Working Group · 2023

Understanding WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.8: Target Size (Minimum)

W3C's explanation of the AA 24 by 24 CSS pixel pointer target rule.

Pointer targets must be at least 24 by 24 CSS px. A smaller target still passes if a 24px circle centered on it doesn't overlap another target or another small target's circle. Inline links in text and equivalent alternatives are exempt. It is the legal floor; Apple's 44pt is the comfortable goal.

Understanding WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.5: Target Size (Enhanced)

W3C's explanation of the AAA 44 by 44 CSS pixel target rule.

At Level AAA, pointer targets must be at least 44 by 44 CSS px, matching Apple's 44pt guidance. Use it as the target for touch-first and comfortable density modes.

W3C Accessibility Guidelines Working Group · 2023

Understanding WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.10: Reflow

W3C's explanation of the AA rule that content reflows without two-way scrolling.

Content must work at a width of 320 CSS px (or a height of 256 CSS px for horizontal text) without scrolling in two directions. This equals 400% zoom on a 1280px viewport. Data tables, maps and toolbars are exempt. It requires layouts built from flexible primitives rather than fixed widths.

W3C Accessibility Guidelines Working Group · 2023

Understanding WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.12: Text Spacing

W3C's explanation of the AA rule that layouts survive user text-spacing overrides.

Nothing may be lost when users set line height to 1.5 times the font size, paragraph spacing to 2 times, letter spacing to 0.12 times and word spacing to 0.16 times. In practice, avoid fixed heights and clipped overflow on any text container.

W3C Accessibility Guidelines Working Group · 2023

Understanding WCAG 2.2 SC 2.4.13: Focus Appearance

W3C's explanation of the AAA minimum size and contrast for focus indicators.

A focus indicator must be at least as large as a 2 CSS px border around the unfocused component, and its pixels must change by at least 3:1 between focused and unfocused states. Although it is AAA, it is the clearest published spec for a focus ring, so we adopt it as our default.
