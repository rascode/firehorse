01 Page patterns

Whole compositions, taken from pages that work

Each one was recorded from a real page before it was rebuilt, so the proportions are measured, not remembered. Keep what the rules say to keep; change the rest.

Fender

A long page that changes gear partway. A note closes the first chapter and opens a tinted band that hangs down the rest of the page, the way a fender hangs down the side of a saddle: through the next section, under its full-width content and through a tall footer to the bottom.

Use it for
Docs, product and service pages that move from showing to explaining: a gallery, then a second chapter; a feature tour, then pricing.
Built from
PNL offset panel, ruled sections, .fh-footer--tall. On a docs page, "panel": true in the page meta.
Keep
One panel per page. Width 52rem from the content edge. The fill is the canvas at 62%, so art behind the page shows only on the open side. Dashed edges, no bottom edge. Nothing sits under the edge line: content is either inset inside the panel or full width across it.
Change freely
Where it starts. The theme, tones and content. The footer's tail (--fh-footer-tail). The panel's width (--fh-panel-size), if it still leaves a clear open side.
Phones
The panel stops 3rem short of the right edge (--fh-panel-gutter), so the split still shows; inset content stays inside it.

Cinch

A page's opening argument, right under its sub-nav: the claim on the left, the substance on the right, held together by one line the way a cinch holds both sides of a saddle. Two panes, two tones, one number to end on.

Use it for
The top of a service, product or assessment page, under the sub-nav: why it matters, and what you get.
Built from
ESP etched split with --roomy, eyebrow pills, fh-heading, fh-lede. In a flush section, full bleed.
Keep
Full bleed, split exactly in half. Copy lines up with the content edge (the panes' outer padding is the page margin). Each pane gets the full section rhythm top and bottom. Two different tones. Claim on the left, facts on the right. One number.
Change freely
The words, the tones, the fact. A checklist on the right instead of the lede, as long as one fact still closes it.
Phones
The panes stack; the line becomes a dashed rule between them.

02 Building blocks

The pieces patterns are cut from

Section-sized layouts you can use on their own. Patterns combine them with the frame and fix their proportions.

Offset panel

A tinted column that begins where you mark it and runs to the bottom of the page, through every section below and the footer. Its fill against the open canvas splits the page in two tones; its dashed edges run the full height. Content stepping onto it takes .fh-panel-inset. Fender is built on it.

Etched split

A full-bleed pair that breaks out of the content column while its copy still lines up with the content edge on both sides. Stacks when the page is narrow. --roomy gives each pane the full section rhythm. Cinch is built on it.

Why it matters

Most systems fail at the seams, not the center

Each tool works on its own. The cost shows up where data crosses from one to the next: re-keyed orders, stale reports, follow-ups nobody owns.

The approach

  • Map every hand-off between systems
  • Fix the model before adding automation

CTA band

The closing ask. A display-size title and the actions on the left; the proof on the right behind a dashed seam: what it costs and takes, who you'll talk to, one line of fine print. A short tone bar rides the top edge. Stacks when the slot is narrow; label, heading, lede and actions on their own still make the simple band.
